Model limits & references

Teaching prototype, not a camera-lens calibration or optical-design certification. First-order calculations: DCC-Lab RayTracing. Spherical surface intersections and refraction: RayOptics.

Stop is at the front vertex; clear lens diameter can further limit the pupil. Focus moves the sensor, not internal lens groups. Focal length scales the prescription at 550 nm. These glass presets are illustrative prescriptions, not measured photographic lenses. Focal length and f-number alone do not determine a real lens's physical length or front-element size.

Target range is straight-line distance from the entrance-pupil centre. X/Y field angles set direction; changing range scales the complete position vector along that direction. Focus, conjugates and DOF use depth along the optical axis, not radial range. Target size is fixed, so apparent size still changes with distance.

The focus sheet is a flat first-order reference. Actual lenses can have a curved best-focus surface depending on their design (field curvature), not necessarily a sphere of equal distance. That curved surface is not calculated here. Infinity focus has no finite object-space sheet.

The DOF volume and visible focus-plane patch are clipped to the full sensor's first-order field of view, including sensor shift. Inspection crops do not change this physical field. Occlusion, vignetting and partially overlapping edge blur are not used to trim the volume. An infinite far limit is shown with an open display cutoff.

The 2D scene uses finite-aperture, first-order projection. It excludes glass aberrations, distortion, reflections, coatings, polarization, sensor microlenses, demosaicing and calibrated noise. Panel diffraction uses a Gaussian central-lobe approximation. Point C uses geometric pupil blur convolved with ideal circular Airy intensity, including rings, and pixel-area integration. That is not a coherent defocused or aberrated wavefront PSF; non-circular pupil diffraction is not calculated.

Stopping down reduces geometric defocus but increases diffraction. Pixel pitch changes sampling, not geometric depth of field unless the blur criterion is tied to pixels.

Exposure grain is a generic signal-dependent shot/read-noise approximation, not measured Canon sensor data. ISO is gain, not extra collected light. Auto brightness gives an illustrative equal-brightness noise comparison. Grain is fixed per photograph and averaged for downsampled previews; dark current, hot pixels and temperature effects are not modeled.

Overview compresses object distances and transverse size; use Lens bench for true-scale glass geometry. Drawn ray density is illustrative, not irradiance.
